### Absorption and formulations

The model includes oral solution and tablet applications. The capsule study is simulated with the tablet particle-size distribution. Solution scenarios were used to separate systemic disposition from dissolution-limited tablet absorption. Tablet absorption was described with formulation-specific dissolution and particle-size assumptions, with fed-state scenarios used to describe the reduced and delayed absorption observed for weak-base ketoconazole under altered gastrointestinal conditions [Marok 2023](#5), Table 1.

Fed-state settings are a gastric emptying time of 45 min and the fed intestinal permeability. The model publication applied them to studies with reported food intake. As an assumption, it also applied them when the food state was not reported and the observed T<sub>max</sub> was more than 2 h, when more than one dose was given per day, or when a single tablet dose was 800 mg or higher [Marok 2023](#5). Four profiles that [Table 2](#table-2) labels as fasted use the fed gastric emptying time: [Boyce 2012](#5) (9) and (10), [Daneshmend 1984](#5) 800 mg (23), and [Wire 2007](#5) (94). Three of these profiles also use the fed intestinal permeability. [Boyce 2012](#5) (10) uses the fasted intestinal permeability. The twice-daily profiles [Daneshmend 1983](#5) (29) and [Patel 2011](#5) (65) use fasted settings.

### Distribution

Ketoconazole is a weak base with high plasma protein binding and pH-dependent solubility. Distribution parameters were based on physicochemical properties and PBPK tissue partitioning methods, with optimized lipophilicity where required to reproduce systemic exposure. The metabolites were represented as separate compounds with their own physicochemical properties and protein-binding assumptions [Marok 2023](#5), Table 1.

### Metabolism, transport and inhibition

Ketoconazole metabolism includes formation of N-deacetylketoconazole by arylacetamide deacetylase and further metabolism through CYP3A4 and UGT pathways. Ketoconazole and metabolites were represented as CYP3A4 and P-gp (ABCB1) inhibitors. In the model publication, this structure described DDI perpetrator behavior more accurately than a parent-only inhibition model [Marok 2023](#5), Table 1, and [Weiss 2022](#5).
